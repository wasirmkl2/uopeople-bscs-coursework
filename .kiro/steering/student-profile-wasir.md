## Writing voice — real examples from this student's own edits

Beyond the general tone rules in `assignment-writing-rules.md`, this student has directly
requested specific paragraph-opening styles across multiple rounds of edits on UNIV 1002-01
Unit 8 discussion work. Use these as concrete before/after examples of the preferred voice,
not just the general principle:

- Avoid opening a paragraph with a flat restatement of the topic ("Stress management matters
  because...") in favor of leading with a consequence or concrete claim first, then landing
  on the point ("If stress goes unmanaged, it doesn't just disappear on its own, it builds up
  over time... That's exactly why stress management matters.").
- Avoid warm, evaluative lead-ins before getting to substance (e.g., "I really like how you
  described that 'boiling feeling'...") in peer replies — this reads as a smoothed-over AI
  tell. Prefer leading directly with the observation itself and folding the reaction in more
  plainly (e.g., "That 'boiling feeling' you mentioned is a good way to describe it.").
- This student's real sentences mix short, plain, direct statements with occasional longer
  ones — not uniformly polished or evenly balanced in length. When asked to "change the
  opening" or "refine," prefer the plainer, more direct option over the more elaborate one
  when given a choice between two drafted alternatives.
- When asked to bold/highlight vocabulary words in a discussion post, this student wants the
  words marked directly in the version presented in chat (even though this may need to be
  manually reapplied in the actual forum's rich-text editor, since Markdown asterisks don't
  render as bold in a plain textbox).

## Outside AI reviews / detector scores — treat with skepticism, verify before responding

This student has, more than once, brought outputs from other AI tools (a second LLM's
rubric self-grade, QuillBot's AI-detection score) to check against finished drafts. Real
examples from this project's history:

- Three different outside "rubric checks" run on the exact same 503-word Unit 8 draft
  reported three different word counts (506, 446, and one that gave no number) and two
  different vocabulary counts (8+, 6) — none of which matched the real, programmatically
  verified count. Always recount word count and vocabulary usage directly against the
  actual saved/approved text before responding to any claim like this, per Section 6 of
  `assignment-writing-rules.md` — do not take the outside tool's number at face value even
  as a starting point.
- A QuillBot "100% AI" flag on a 96-110 word peer reply was raised as a concern. AI-detection
  tools are especially unreliable on very short text and cannot be reasoned about
  quantitatively (see Section 6's existing guidance) — the response should be a genuine,
  specific critique of the actual prose for real AI-pattern tells (e.g., neat either/or
  rhetorical questions, smooth evaluative lead-ins, uniform sentence rhythm), not a
  restatement that detectors are unreliable followed by silence. Never suggest deliberately
  degrading grammar or inserting errors to lower a detector score — this was explicitly
  rejected as an approach in this project.
