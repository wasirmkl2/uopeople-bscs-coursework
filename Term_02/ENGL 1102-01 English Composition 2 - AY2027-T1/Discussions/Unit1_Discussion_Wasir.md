# Discussion Forum Unit 1: Pre-Writing Strategies and the Scientific Method

**Posted by:** S M Wasir Jayed Rafi
**Course:** ENGL 1102-01 — English Composition 2 (AY2027-T1)

I've wanted to build something that genuinely changes how people work or live for a while now, and lately that ambition has narrowed down to AI engineering specifically. Before writing this post, I opened Google Keep and jotted down a quick list to get my thoughts organized rather than just diving in blind. Hall and Wallace (2018) describe brainstorming as one of the simplest prewriting tools available, since it lets a writer get ideas onto the page before worrying about how they connect (Section 4.1). My list started scattered: "AI Engineer," "Copilot/ChatGPT while coding," "does it really help me learn?," "job postings," "math I'm missing." Looking back at it, the note wasn't really random, it kept circling one specific worry: whether leaning on AI coding assistants while I'm still learning to program is helping me understand code or just helping me finish it faster.

That worry turned into my research question once I applied the scientific method Serdikoff (2021) lays out: does using an AI coding assistant while learning to program help or hurt a beginner's real understanding of the code, compared to writing it without help? My hypothesis is that leaning on AI assistance would get the code working faster, but my grasp of that code would end up shallower than if I worked through it on my own, since accepting a suggestion is easy, and skipping the step of tracing why it works is even easier.

Testing this would mean picking problems that are roughly equal in difficulty, keeping the count and time limit the same on both sides, and switching up which condition goes first so any improvement from practice doesn't get mistaken for improvement from AI help. One set I'd work through with an AI assistant open; the other, completely alone. Once both were done, I'd quiz myself, explaining the logic out loud and tweaking each solution slightly, then compare how confident I actually felt doing that for each set. Serdikoff (2021) describes scientific research as a systematic process of asking a question and then gathering evidence to check it, which is exactly what this comparison would let me do instead of just assuming AI help is good or bad.

What I like about running my topic through this process is that it turned a vague ambition, wanting to build something impactful, into a specific, checkable question about how I'm learning right now. Brainstorming got the raw material out of my head, but the scientific method is what forced me to turn that material into something I could test rather than just believe. If I run this comparison, I'll know sooner whether my current study habits are building the kind of depth an AI engineer trying to build something significant is going to need, instead of finding out the hard way later.
