# Discussion Forum Unit 7: Composing an Abstract for the AI Coding Assistants in CS1 Study

**Posted by:** S M Wasir Jayed Rafi
**Course:** ENGL 1102-01 — English Composition 2 (AY2027-T1)

Here is the abstract for my paper on AI coding assistants in CS1. Serdikoff's (2021) sample abstract states a research question and explains why it is worth investigating (p. 122), and the Writing Center (n.d.) says an abstract should make sense on its own and usually leaves out citations, so I kept them out of the abstract below.

**AI Coding Assistants and CS1 Learning Outcomes: A Proposed Follow-Up Study**

AI coding assistants such as GitHub Copilot are becoming common in first programming courses (CS1). Earlier studies suggest that novices who use them finish more tasks and often score higher during the course, and students rate their own learning positively. However, the five studies reviewed measure outcomes only inside the course or soon after students start using the tool. None shows whether the advantage survives once the assistant is gone, or whether students' confidence matches what they can do alone. That gap matters because instructors, bootcamps, and employers could mistake assisted performance for independent ability. This study aims to establish whether the early benefits of AI-assisted CS1 teaching turn into lasting programming skill. Three objectives guide it: evaluating whether in-course advantages persist on an AI-free assessment one semester later, comparing the conceptual understanding of students who used an assistant on three or more graded assignments with that of peers who did not, and testing whether self-rated learning predicts later unaided performance. The research questions ask how far the advantages last, how conceptual understanding differs between the groups, and how well self-ratings predict unaided results. A mixed-methods design is proposed. The quantitative strand compares both groups on an identical, AI-free concept check, and the qualitative strand uses interviews with a handful of students to explore how they coped without the tool. The study hasn't been carried out, so no findings exist yet. If the advantage fades, courses may need to pair AI use with unaided practice; if it lasts, instructors could adopt these tools with more confidence.

I waited until the Unit 5 design was settled to write this, since the same guide (The Writing Center, n.d.) advises drafting the abstract after the full paper so there is something real to summarize.
